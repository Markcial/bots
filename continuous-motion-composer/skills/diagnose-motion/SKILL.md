# Diagnose the motion brief

Use for every new motion request, and again when the scope of an approved project changes. Drawing and animation do not start until the user has approved the result.

## Steps

1. Read any file or link the user already shared (a script, character sheet, brand tokens, voice, music, previous frames). Say what you found. Do not invent the rest.
2. Ask for what is missing in each of the seven areas. Ask one short question per gap.
   - **Brief:** what is being made, what it is for, and what success looks like.
   - **Audience:** who sees it, where, and at what size or distance.
   - **Aspect ratios:** 16:9, 9:16, both, or another size the owner names. Do not add a ratio they did not ask for.
   - **Length:** the target duration, and whether the picture times to narration.
   - **Brand tokens:** colours, type, logo rules and any motion rules the owner already has. If a design teammate keeps a brand book, read it and do not re-ask what it answers. If tokens are missing, say so; propose neutrals only if you label them as a proposal.
   - **Existing assets:** characters, logos, stills, fonts, previous frames, and what must stay.
   - **Voice or music source:** none; files the owner will provide; or an audio teammate (Computerized Audio Producer when it is installed). If voice will arrive later, say the final render waits for the sync unless the owner asks for a silent or temporary cut.
3. Write the diagnosis in one page: one short section per area, then the tools you will use (Remotion by default; Blender only if the brief needs real 3D and it is installed), the cost (zero unless you are about to suggest a paid tool), the risks and open questions, and what you will deliver if it is approved.
4. If a step would use a paid image-to-video or text-to-video service, write the cost on this page and do not treat that tool as the plan until the owner says yes. Free tools first.
5. Save it as a memory note titled `diagnosis-<project-slug>` with the status line `awaiting OK`.
6. Send it to the user and ask for an OK or changes. Stop.
7. On an explicit OK, set the status to `approved <date>`. Say what you will do next (the storyboard).

## Do not

- Sketch, generate, pick a palette, or render anything before the OK.
- Count silence, a thumbs-up on something else, or "sounds good" about another message as approval.
- Fill a missing area with your own assumption without saying so.
- Promise a camera move, a morph or a physical stunt the chosen tools cannot make.
